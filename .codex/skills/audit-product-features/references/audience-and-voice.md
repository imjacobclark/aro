# Aro Audience and Voice

## Primary audience

Write first for people who have built a personal music collection and want it
to feel valuable again. They keep albums as files—often lossless or
high-resolution—on a Mac, external drive, home computer, or private storage.
They may own a good pair of headphones, speakers, DAC, or hi-fi system, but
they should not need to understand networking or database technology.

They care about:

- owning their music instead of depending on a subscription catalogue
- hearing their files at their proper quality
- browsing albums and artists as a collection, not an endless feed
- reaching the same library from another device at home
- keeping chosen music available when storage or networking is unavailable
- knowing originals will not be altered, recompressed, or unexpectedly deleted
- finding duplicates, missing files, and forgotten favourites
- escaping fiddly setup and invisible background failures

Secondary audiences include households with one shared collection, collectors
with a home music server, and listeners moving away from a streaming-only
library.

## Positioning

Aro is the private home for a music collection: a native Mac player that
preserves the owner's files, gives capable audio equipment the right signal,
and makes one personal library available across the owner's devices.

The strongest themes are:

1. **Ownership without compromise** — The collection remains the listener's,
   in its original files and quality.
2. **Hi-fi made approachable** — Serious playback choices are presented as
   clear benefits, not laboratory controls.
3. **One collection around the home** — Personal music follows the listener
   without becoming a public cloud library.
4. **Confidence over housekeeping** — Aro watches, verifies, protects,
   diagnoses, and recovers so the collection feels dependable.
5. **Rediscovery** — Browsing, history, and statistics turn stored files back
   into an active relationship with music.

## Voice

Sound confident, calm, informed, and human. Appeal to care for a collection
without gatekeeping or hi-fi snobbery.

Prefer:

- “Hear every song at its native quality.”
- “Your originals stay exactly as they are.”
- “Take favourite albums with you.”
- “See what you have been listening to.”
- “Connect another device with a six-digit code.”

Avoid:

- “Leverage a content-addressed media store.”
- “Deploy an authoritative synchronization hub.”
- “Built for serious audiophiles only.”
- “Revolutionary bit-perfect technology.”
- “Seamlessly optimize your listening workflow.”

Name the emotional or practical outcome first. Mention a mechanism only when
it helps the customer trust the promise.

## Claim standard

A strong feature bullet is specific enough to be believable and broad enough
to matter:

```markdown
- **Originals Stay Original** — Aro preserves the exact audio files you add, without recompressing, retagging, or changing their format.
```

A weak bullet merely exposes implementation:

```markdown
- **SHA-256 Blob Verification** — Every downloaded blob is checked by hash.
```

A weak bullet also overstates evidence:

```markdown
- **Your Music Everywhere** — Stream from any device anywhere in the world.
```

When evidence supports a constraint, turn it into honest positioning. “Around
your home” is stronger than an unsupported promise of access everywhere.
